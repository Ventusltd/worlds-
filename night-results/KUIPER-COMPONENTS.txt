THE KUIPER PART LIST
======================================================================

Every electrical part that can be described by a kind and a size,
worked out in advance, with the ones worth offering kept. Reading
this needs no program, no account and nobody's help.

THE KINDS OF PART THERE ARE. Everything electrical is built from
these, and there are not many:

  resists                        measured in ohm         turns current into heat; the commonest part there is
  stores in a magnetic field     measured in henry       opposes a CHANGE in current
  stores in an electric field    measured in farad       opposes a CHANGE in voltage
  pushes at a fixed voltage      measured in volt        a supply that holds its voltage
  pushes a fixed current         measured in amp         a supply that holds its current
  lets current one way           measured in volt        a one-way valve, with a small drop
  opens and closes               measured in ohm         nearly nothing closed, nearly everything open
  changes voltage                measured in ratio       more turns one side than the other
  a length of wire               measured in ohm per km  resists a little and stores a little
  draws power                    measured in watt        whatever the system exists to feed

THE SIZES THEY COME IN. Parts are not made in every size. They are
made in twelve steps that repeat every time the numbers get ten
times bigger, so the gap between one size and the next is always
about the same PROPORTION rather than the same amount. That is why
the same twelve numbers keep appearing everywhere:

  1.0  1.2  1.5  1.8  2.2  2.7  3.3  3.9  4.7  5.6  6.8  8.2

WHERE THESE COME FROM, and why that is the important part

Nothing here is invented. The line, transformer and fuse types are
the standard types published by an open library that anyone may
download and check for themselves:

  taken from    pandapower basic_std_types
  version       3.5.4
  licence       open source, BSD-3-Clause
  types used    106

The conductor sizes are the British standard series - the sizes
actually manufactured, in square millimetres:

  1.5  2.5  4  6  10  16  25  35  50  70  95  120  150  185  240  300  400  500  630  800  1000

The protective ratings are the British standard series too, in amps:

  6  10  16  20  25  32  40  50  63  80  100  125  160  200  250  315  400  500  630  800  1000  1250

And the voltages are the ones this country actually runs at, from
the socket in a wall to the highest transmission line, in volts:

  230  400  3300  6600  11000  33000  66000  132000  275000  400000

WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN IT SOUNDS. Elsewhere in this work, seven
hundred and forty-nine figures were found resting on eighteen
values that could not be traced to any document at all. Every
figure in this list can be traced to something a stranger can go
and obtain. That is the difference between a catalogue and a guess,
and it is why the source and its version are printed above rather
than being left as a claim that these are 'standard'.

WHY TEN THOUSAND AND NOT ALL OF THEM. Offering a million choices is
the same as offering none. The kept ones are spread EVENLY across
the whole range rather than taken from its best end, so that picking
any one of them tells you something the next one would not have. A
chooser that only offers the edges of a range teaches nothing about
its middle.

HOW TO ADD A PART OF YOUR OWN

Four things on one line, separated by spaces:

  a name you invent | the kind, from the list above | its number | its unit

For example:

  my-feeder-cable   a length of wire   0.164   ohm per km
  my-supply         pushes at a fixed voltage   400   volt

That is the whole format. The parts listed above are stored exactly
that way - there is no privileged format and no hidden one - so a
part you write is drawn and worked out identically to a part that
came with the list. Nothing you add is treated as second class.
