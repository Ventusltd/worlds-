THE KUIPER NETWORK TABLE
======================================================================

Every way to wire a network, and what each way costs you. Worked out
in advance, so that reading this needs no program, no account and
nobody's help. If you are holding this and everyone who made it is
gone, it still answers.

THE SAME QUESTION EVERYWHERE. A processor's cores must reach each
other. A solar farm's panels must reach a substation. A country's
power stations must reach its cities. These look like different
problems and they are one problem: things that must reach each
other, joined by links, where links cost money and failures are
certain. The shapes below are the whole of the choice, and each one
fixes four things by arithmetic the moment you pick it.

  LINKS       how many joins you must pay for and maintain
  WORST TRIP  the longest journey between any two points, in hops
  SURVIVES    how many links can fail before something is cut off
  SPLIT       how many links must be cut to halve the network -
              which is what limits how much can flow across it

HOW TO READ THAT

A line and a star both cost the fewest links, and both SURVIVE
NOTHING - one failure anywhere cuts somebody off. That is why they
are cheap. A ring costs one more link than a line and survives one
failure, which is the cheapest step from fragile to not fragile, and
it is why so much of the world is wired in rings.

Everything joined to everything survives almost any failure and has
the shortest trip possible, and nobody builds it, because the links
grow with the SQUARE of the number of points. Double the network and
you quadruple the bill.

The shapes in between - grids, wrapped grids, folded cubes - exist
because they buy short trips and real survivability without that
square. The folded cube is what large processors use. The wrapped
grid is what large machine rooms use. Power grids use rings and
meshes for the same reason and call it something else.

HOW TO DISBELIEVE THIS PROPERLY

Every number was worked out twice, by two different routes to the
same quantity. Where they disagreed nothing was published for that
shape - not a warning, not a footnote. They disagreed on 0.

524,288 shape-and-size combinations were worked out, up to networks of
65,537 points.

WHAT IS NOT HERE. These are the shapes themselves, counted exactly.
They say nothing about whether a link can carry what you intend to
put through it, what it costs in your currency, or whether the
failures you actually get are the ones counted here. A shape that
survives two failures survives two RANDOM ones; it may not survive
two that happen for the same reason, and in real life that is the
usual case.
